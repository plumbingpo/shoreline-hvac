A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In Shoreline, WA, the failures cluster at predictable points: an oxide-coated flame sensor, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

Rather than guess, Shoreline HVAC tests the sequence stage by stage. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: we inspect the heat exchanger, leak-test the gas connections, and verify flue draft. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so airflow and flame are matched as the manufacturer intended.

We work on gas, propane, and electric models from every major brand, both conventional 80 percent units and condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drains, and dual pressure switches. The truck stock covers the parts that actually fail: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most repairs finish in one trip. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Shoreline

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue
  • The furnace clicks and glows but the burners never light
  • Squeals, scrapes, or grinding noises behind the blower door
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Repair?

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

At Shoreline HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, keeps causing trouble. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to catch airflow problems early, and test the gas side with a leak detector, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
