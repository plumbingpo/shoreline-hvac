Where do most HVAC installations go wrong? Usually before the first wrench turns, when someone matches the new tonnage to the old nameplate and skips the math. At Shoreline HVAC, the math comes first. We build a load calculation for your Shoreline, WA home from its square footage, insulation levels, windows, orientation, and air leakage, because a system sized by habit rather than by measurement is wrong from day one. From there the job becomes three connected pieces of work: equipment matched to the load, ductwork able to deliver what that equipment produces, and a commissioning process that proves the result on the meter instead of on paper.

Once the load is known, we select equipment to fit it, and we pick it as a matched set. Manufacturers rate the outdoor unit, indoor coil, and blower together, so a mismatched pairing quietly gives up the SEER2 and AFUE figures printed on the sticker. You will get a plain-language explanation of what single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed equipment actually do, where an ECM blower earns its cost, and whether a furnace, air handler, or heat pump makes the most sense for your house, with no pressure attached. The ducts get the same scrutiny. Our experienced technicians measure static pressure, track down leaky or undersized runs, and fix the chokepoints while everything is open, since new equipment on bad ducts never reaches its rating.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a fast one part ways. Our technicians pressure-test and evacuate the line set to a micron-verified vacuum, weigh in the charge and prove it with superheat and subcooling readings, leak-test the gas side, vent to the manufacturer's spec, and trap the condensate with float-switch protection. The thermostat gets configured for your equipment's real staging rather than left on defaults. Then comes full commissioning: temperature split, temperature rise, amp draws, and static pressure, all recorded and explained to you before we haul the old system away. Why Professional HVAC Installation Matters

An HVAC system is the biggest machine in most homes, and installation is where its entire service life gets decided. The failure modes are invisible at handoff: a vacuum never verified means moisture breeding acid in the refrigerant loop, a charge set by feel means efficiency lost for good, mismatched coil-and-condenser pairings mean the rated numbers were never achievable, and duct restrictions mean the blower spends fifteen years straining against static pressure it was never designed for. Refrigerant work is federally regulated and gas and venting mistakes are safety problems, not just performance problems.

What sets a professional install apart is that every step gets verified before the next one begins. We size from a load calculation, install rated combinations, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, weigh the charge and confirm it with superheat and subcooling, leak-test the gas piping, verify draft, and set blower speed to hold the rated temperature rise. Commissioning then records the numbers, giving every future service call a baseline to compare against. Those recorded measurements are the difference between a system that holds its rated efficiency for fifteen years and one that merely runs, and we do it on every installation.

Maintenance Tips for HVAC Installation

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Flush the drain line before summer to keep algae from clogging it
  • Leave supply and return vents open so the system stays balanced the way it was commissioned
  • Replace filters on time from the first month so the coil and exchanger never go short on airflow
  • Keep the startup readings on file, since they speed up every future diagnosis
  • Give the outdoor unit clear space on every side and a gentle rinse each season
  • Deal with minor rattles and comfort changes before they grow into repairs
  • Keep a spring and fall maintenance visit on the calendar every year
